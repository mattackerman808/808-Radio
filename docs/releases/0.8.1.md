# 808 Radio 0.8.1

**An FM + HD Radio receiver for Windows and macOS with RTL-SDR dongles, styled as a car stereo.**

![808 Radio](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/raw/v0.8.1/docs/images/808radio.png)

## What it is

808 Radio turns a $30 RTL-SDR dongle into a proper radio: FM stereo with RDS, and **HD Radio** (NRSC-5) with album
art, multicast channels (HD1–HD8), weather and traffic maps, blended with the analog signal like a real HD receiver.
It finds the best gain for each station by itself and corrects the dongle's frequency error automatically.

The dongle can be on the computer or on a Raspberry Pi near the antenna running the standard `rtl_tcp` server, which
808 Radio finds on the network by itself.

## How it works

- **The receiver** is C#: the RTL-SDR's samples are filtered, demodulated to FM stereo with RDS, and the HD Radio
  sidebands go to [nrsc5](https://github.com/theori-io/nrsc5) for decoding. The analog and digital audio are blended
  the way a car receiver does it, so a weak HD station falls back to analog instead of dropping out.
- **The faceplate** is a car stereo: a dot-matrix display, HD program keys, presets, a tuning knob with detents.
  Press OPEN and it folds down to an instrument panel drawn at the display's refresh rate: spectrum and waterfall
  with click-to-tune, the FM multiplex, and live receiver statistics.
- **The weather map** is the station's own radar image (HD Radio's HERE image service) drawn over a street map of
  the same box from OpenStreetMap data, cached so it works without the internet.
- Windows builds use Windows Forms with Direct2D for the panel; macOS uses Avalonia. The receiver is the same code.

![The instrument panel behind the faceplate](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/raw/v0.8.1/docs/images/808radio-open.png)

## What's new in 0.8.1

**HD locks on stations next to a strong neighbour.**

- **The automatic gain now steers by the station's spectrum.** It used to raise the tuner gain until the dongle's
  ADC started to clip, then back off. But beside a strong station the tuner itself overloads well before that: the
  noise floor rises faster than the gain while nothing clips, and the HD sidebands sink into it. 104.9 next to the
  105.3–105.7 cluster ended up near 20 dB, where its HD never synced at all; its peak is 7.7 dB. The gain is now
  peaked on what the station needs: its HD sidebands over the noise floor across the dongle's whole window once it
  shows them, the analog carrier otherwise, with clipping still the ceiling. The sidebands are visible before HD
  syncs, so there's nothing to wait for. 104.9 now syncs in about five seconds at MER 9–10 dB, and stations that were
  already fine land on the same peak as before.
- **Faster HD tracking.** Until HD decodes, the gain is re-checked every few seconds (it was about fifteen) for the
  first minute on a station, and once HD is decoding the gain follows nrsc5's MER as before.
- Tuning to a very strong station at a high gain no longer leaves the gain to the slow tracking: the search starts
  over after the overload guard steps it down.

All of it is on both platforms.

## Download

- **Windows 10/11 (x64)**: `808Radio-v0.8.1-win-x64.zip`. Unzip anywhere and run `808Radio.exe`. Self-contained:
  no installer, no .NET to install. The dongle needs the WinUSB driver once (Zadig); see the README.
- **macOS (Apple Silicon)**: `808Radio-v0.8.1-mac-arm64.zip`. Unzip and open `808 Radio.app`. Signed and notarized,
  with the native libraries inside; a USB dongle needs no driver on macOS.

Upgrading keeps your settings and presets (`%APPDATA%\808Radio` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/808Radio`
on the Mac).

Full details in [CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/blob/v0.8.1/CHANGELOG.md) and the
[README](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/blob/v0.8.1/README.md).
