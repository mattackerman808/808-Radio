# 808 Radio 0.8.2

**An FM + HD Radio receiver for Windows and macOS with RTL-SDR dongles, styled as a car stereo.**

![808 Radio](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/raw/v0.8.2/docs/images/808radio.png)

## What it is

808 Radio turns a $30 RTL-SDR dongle into a proper radio: FM stereo with RDS, and **HD Radio** (NRSC-5) with album
art, multicast channels (HD1–HD8), weather and traffic maps, blended with the analog signal like a real HD receiver.
It finds the best gain for each station by itself and corrects the dongle's frequency error automatically.

The dongle can be on the computer or on a Raspberry Pi near the antenna running the standard `rtl_tcp` server, which
808 Radio finds on the network by itself.

## How it works

- **The receiver** is C#: the RTL-SDR's samples are filtered, demodulated to FM stereo with RDS, and the HD Radio
  sidebands go to [nrsc5](https://github.com/theori-io/nrsc5) for decoding. The analog and digital audio are blended
  the way a car receiver does it, so a weak HD station falls back to analog instead of dropping out.
- **The faceplate** is a car stereo: a dot-matrix display, HD program keys, presets, a tuning knob with detents.
  Press OPEN and it folds down to an instrument panel drawn at the display's refresh rate: spectrum and waterfall
  with click-to-tune, the FM multiplex, and live receiver statistics.
- **The weather map** is the station's own radar image (HD Radio's HERE image service) drawn over a street map of
  the same box from OpenStreetMap data, cached so it works without the internet.
- Windows builds use Windows Forms with Direct2D for the panel; macOS uses Avalonia. The receiver is the same code.

![The instrument panel behind the faceplate](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/raw/v0.8.2/docs/images/808radio-open.png)

## What's new in 0.8.2

**The automatic gain finds a better spot on weak HD stations, and stays off the edge next to strong ones.**

- **The top of a flat peak, not the bottom.** On a weak HD station the signal over the noise barely changes across a
  wide range of gain, but HD reception still improves a little with it. 0.8.1 took the lowest gain that was as good
  as the best, which left 91.1 at 14.4 dB, 0.2-0.3 dB of MER short of 20.7. It now takes the highest such gain that
  is two steps under clipping, as before 0.8.1.
- **Headroom under a strong neighbour's overload.** Beside a strong station the tuner overloads at some gain while
  the ADC doesn't clip, and HD falls off a cliff above it (104.9 without a masthead amp: MER 7.8 at 12.5 dB, 5.8 at
  14.4, 2.5 at 15.7). The gain now stays two steps under where that starts, since the edge moves with the
  neighbour's audio. A sharp peak stays where it is: 104.9 with the amp still lands on 7.7 dB.

All of it is on both platforms.

## Download

- **Windows 10/11 (x64)**: `808Radio-v0.8.2-win-x64.zip`. Unzip anywhere and run `808Radio.exe`. Self-contained:
  no installer, no .NET to install. The dongle needs the WinUSB driver once (Zadig); see the README.
- **macOS (Apple Silicon)**: `808Radio-v0.8.2-mac-arm64.zip`. Unzip and open `808 Radio.app`. Signed and notarized,
  with the native libraries inside; a USB dongle needs no driver on macOS.

Upgrading keeps your settings and presets (`%APPDATA%\808Radio` on Windows, `~/Library/Application Support/808Radio`
on the Mac).

Full details in [CHANGELOG.md](https://github.com/mattackerman808/808-Radio/blob/v0.8.2/CHANGELOG.md) and the
